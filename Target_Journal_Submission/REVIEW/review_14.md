# Review round 14 — Associate Editor, after revision

**Fit and contribution.** The paper is now an empirical-and-theoretical study with a clear practical message,
supported by a pre-registered real-noise experiment. That is unusual in the noisy-label literature and is its
strongest asset. The proposed method is presented honestly, including a real-data failure.

**Risks at review.**
1. *Novelty perception* (MAJOR): a referee may see "regularise, don't relabel" plus a method that fails on real data
   as insufficient. Mitigation: the theory (Propositions 1–2, Corollary 1), the scale of the evidence, and the
   synthetic-vs-real contrast are new; the cover letter must say so plainly.
2. *Length* (MODERATE): about 16,000 words including tables, 10 figures and 13 tables. Acceptable for a full research
   article, but referees may ask to move material to the supplement; four figures and one table have already been moved.
3. *Scope of real-world evidence* (MODERATE): one dataset.
4. *Formatting* (MODERATE): the live JKSUCIS guideline page could not be fetched; reference style and declaration
   wording need a final check by the authors.
5. *Author-side items* (BLOCKING until done): authors, affiliations, funding, contributions, competing interests,
   AI-use disclosure and a public repository URL.

**Recommendation.** Ready to submit once item 5 is completed. Expected outcome: review, with a realistic chance of
"major revision" requesting more real-noise data or stronger baselines; the paper's own limitations section
anticipates both.
