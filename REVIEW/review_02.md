# Review round 2 — novelty and prior art

| # | Severity | Issue | Action | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MAJOR | Closed-form LOO predictions for ridge on ROCKET features are the core of *prevalidated ridge regression* (Dempster, Webb & Schmidt, arXiv 2401.15610). Is this paper a re-application? | The first topic candidate (LOO-based probability calibration) was **abandoned** for exactly this reason during topic selection. The present paper uses the identity for a different purpose (latent-label EM under noise), cites PreVal as the closest relative in Related work and Discussion, and claims no novelty for the identity itself. | Addressed |
| 2 | MAJOR | EM with a transition matrix is 45 years old (Dawid & Skene 1979; Bootkrajang & Kabán 2012); BIC is standard. What is new? | Contribution statement narrowed to: (i) LOO predictive distribution inside the EM, shown by ablation to be necessary in the p ≫ n regime (in-sample variant is inert); (ii) label-independence of the hat matrix making each iteration a matrix product; (iii) model selection among noise models, shown necessary by the v1 failure. Stated as a combination with a demonstrated reason for each part, not as a new learning principle. | Addressed in text |
| 3 | MAJOR | "Widen the grid" is trivial; is it already known? | Searched (ROCKET/MiniRocket/Hydra papers, aeon defaults, noisy-label TSC literature): the default grid is logspace(−3, 3, 10) throughout and we found no study of it under label noise. The paper says "we found no systematic study", not "nobody has done this". Triviality of the fix is presented as a virtue; the non-trivial parts are the explanation (Prop. 1), the size of the effect and the metric caveat. | Addressed; residual risk noted in final report |
| 4 | MODERATE | Proposition 1 resembles known results on symmetric-noise robustness (Ghosh et al. 2017; Chen et al. 2021). | Both cited; the text states that Prop. 1 is "the analogous statement for the ridge decision function itself". It is exact for finite samples conditional on the features because ridge is linear in the targets, which the cited results are not; this is said without overclaiming. | Addressed |
| 5 | MODERATE | Competing noisy-label TSC methods (SREA, CTW, Scale-teaching, DTW granular balls) are cited but not compared. | Not feasible on the available CPU-only machine and their evaluations use different subsets/splits; said explicitly in Discussion and Limitations. Simple deep baselines (FCN with CE, GCE, Co-teaching) are run on a small subset and described as not supporting general claims. | Disclosed |
| 6 | MODERATE | Two earlier topic candidates were dropped for prior art (PreVal; TS-ACL for exemplar-free continual TSC). Is the surviving topic merely what was left? | Recorded in the journal/topic log; the surviving question (behaviour of the kernel-ridge family under label noise) has a real experimental gap and the answer turned out to be non-obvious (cleaning hurts; metric dependence). | Noted |
| 7 | MINOR | Title promised a method. | Title now states the finding. | Fixed |

Patent search: no patent or technical disclosure specific to label-noise handling for random-convolutional-kernel classifiers was found in a keyword search; generic noisy-label EM is long-standing public art. No novelty is claimed that depends on the absence of patents.
